01 — About

A career built between the lab bench and the gigawatt plant.

Twelve years translating electrochemistry into industrial hydrogen technology — across academia, OEMs, and EU consortia.

Dr. Ansgar Hellström is a PhD researcher and R&D leader specializing in green hydrogen production, alkaline water electrolysis, and industrial electrochemical engineering. He holds a PhD in Electrochemical Engineering from KTH Royal Institute of Technology and currently leads the Cell & Stack Architecture group at ETH Zürich in partnership with Nordic Hydrogen AS.

His research spans the full technology readiness spectrum — from fundamental studies of non-noble electrocatalysts and triple-phase boundaries, to multi-physics stack modeling, to the qualification of 100 MW electrolyser plants for steel, ammonia, and refining off-takers. The common thread: turning electrochemistry into durable, economically viable hardware.

Before returning to academia in 2021, Ansgar spent six years at Siemens Energy leading cell-stack development for the Silyzer PEM platform. That industrial experience shapes how he frames research questions today — a paper is a starting point; a 50 MW plant running at 47 kWh/kgH₂ is the answer.

Research Vision

"To make green hydrogen boringly cheap and reliably durable — so that decarbonizing heavy industry becomes an engineering decision, not a policy debate. Every paper, patent, and stack I design is a step toward that simplicity."

02 — Research Areas

Six domains. One goal: industrial-scale green hydrogen.

From fundamental electrocatalysis to system-level renewable integration — research pillars covering the full green hydrogen value chain.

Green Hydrogen

Production pathways, techno-economic analysis, and policy frameworks for renewable hydrogen at industrial scale. LCOH modeling across European corridors.

LCOH Power-to-X Policy

Alkaline Electrolysers

Zero-gap cell architecture, advanced diaphragms, and Ni-based electrodes for high-current-density operation above 1.5 A/cm² with long-term durability.

AWE Zero-gap Diaphragms

Electrochemical Engineering

Multi-physics modeling of porous electrodes, mass transport in three-phase boundaries, and scale-up principles from cm² lab cells to m² industrial stacks.

Multi-physics Scale-up Transport

Fuel Cells

PEMFC and AEMFC stack development, catalyst layer optimization, and degradation mechanisms under dynamic load — bridging hydrogen production and end-use.

PEMFC AEMFC Catalysts

Hydrogen Storage

Compressed, liquid, and material-based storage. Underground salt cavern modeling and integrated production-storage-offtake system optimization.

Salt caverns Compression Integration

Renewable Energy

Variable renewable coupling, hybrid AC/DC architectures, and grid-balancing strategies for electrolysis plants under photovoltaic and wind input profiles.

PV/Wind Grid balance Hybrid
